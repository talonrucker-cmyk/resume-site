Phase 1 · Build the buckets · Stop 1

Retirement isn’t an age — it’s a number.

You’re buying your salary

Retirement means replacing your paycheck without a job. Your savings have to throw off income for the rest of your life.

Aim to replace ~80%

Most people need roughly 70–85% of their pre-retirement income each year — the house and kids often cost less, but healthcare and free time cost more.

The 25× shortcut

A rough target: 25× the annual income your savings must cover. Need your portfolio to supply $40k a year? You’re aiming at about $1,000,000.

“The question was never ‘what age?’ — it’s ‘what number lets my money outlast me?’”
01 — A NUMBER, NOT AN AGE

Same dollars, wildly different after-tax outcomes — where you save matters almost as much as how much.

The HSA secret

With a high-deductible health plan, the HSA is the only account that’s deductible going in and tax-free coming out. After 65 you can pull it for anything, like an IRA.

02 — THE ACCOUNT LINEUP
Phase 1 · Build the buckets · Stop 3

Pay the tax now, or later?

Traditional = deduct now

Contributions lower this year’s taxable income. You then pay ordinary tax on every dollar you pull out in retirement.

Roth = tax-free later

No deduction today — but decades of growth and every withdrawal come out completely tax-free.

It’s a tax-rate bet

Roth wins if your rate will be higher later; Traditional wins if it’ll be lower. Young or low-income years favor Roth.

“Most people should own some of both — tax diversification lets you choose which bucket to tap each year.”
03 — TRADITIONAL VS ROTH
Phase 1 · Build the buckets · Stop 4

Free money first, then fill in order.

Grab the full match

An employer match is an instant 50–100% return. Contribute at least enough to get all of it — nothing else beats it.

Then debt & Roth

After the match, wipe out high-interest debt, then fund a Roth IRA, then circle back to max the 401(k).

Mind the limits

Each account has a yearly cap. Once you’re 50+, ‘catch-up’ contributions let you add even more.

401(k) employee~$24,500plus a catch-up if you’re 50+.
IRA (Roth or Traditional)~$7,500combined across all your IRAs.
HSA (family)~$8,750with a qualifying high-deductible plan.

06 — ARE YOU ON TRACK?
Phase 3 · Spend it down · Stop 7

Spend 4%, and the odds say it lasts.

Where it comes from

Studies found a portfolio could hand you 4% the first year, raise it with inflation each year after, and survive 30+ years through history’s worst markets.

A guide, not a law

Retire into a crash, live longer, or hold too much cash and the safe number can be lower. Many planners now start nearer 3.5%.

Stay flexible

Trim spending in down years and you can safely spend more in good ones. A rule with a little give beats a rigid one.

“The 4% rule turns a scary pile of money into a simple paycheck — then you adjust as real life happens.”
07 — THE 4% RULE
Phase 3 · Spend it down · Stop 8

A crash early hurts far more than a crash late.

The retiree’s hidden risk

Two retirees can earn the same average return and get opposite outcomes — the one who hits a crash in year one can run out decades sooner.

Selling into a downturn

Pulling income from stocks while they’re down locks in the losses. Those shares are gone — they can never recover for you.

Keep a cash cushion

Hold 1–3 years of spending in cash and bonds. In a downturn you live off that — and give your stocks time to bounce back.

“You can’t control when the market falls — but a cash cushion decides whether it’s a scare or a catastrophe.”
08 — SEQUENCE-OF-RETURNS RISK
Phase 3 · Spend it down · Stop 9

Draw them down in the right order.

A common withdrawal order
1 · Taxable brokerageLow capital-gains rates; let the rest growFirst
2 · Tax-deferred (401k / IRA)Taxed as income; drain before RMDs force itNext
3 · RothTax-free — let it compound as long as possibleLast
Every year · fill low bracketsBlend sources to control your tax billBlend
The order you spend your buckets can add years to how long the money lasts.

Why it works

Spending taxable money first keeps your tax bill low early, lets tax-advantaged accounts grow longer, and saves the tax-free Roth for last — or for heirs.

09 — WHICH ACCOUNT TO TAP FIRST
Phase 3 · Spend it down · Stop 10

At 73, the IRS makes you withdraw.

The forced withdrawal

Starting at 73, you must pull a required minimum from Traditional 401(k)s and IRAs each year — and pay ordinary tax on it, like it or not.

The penalty is steep

Miss an RMD and the penalty runs up to 25% of what you should have taken. Automate it so you never slip.

Roth escapes it

Roth IRAs have no RMDs in your lifetime. Converting to Roth in your 60s can shrink future RMDs and the taxes they trigger.

“RMDs are why your 60s — low-income, pre-RMD years — are prime time to convert to Roth and smooth out the tax hit.”
10 — RMDs AT 73
Phase 4 · Protect the plan · Stop 11

The biggest bill most plans forget.

Before 65: the coverage gap

Retire early and you’re on your own for health insurance until Medicare starts at 65.

  • ●ACA marketplace plans are the usual bridge for those years.
  • ●Keeping taxable income low can earn big premium subsidies — another reason withdrawal order matters.
Health costs are the wildcard that sinks more retirements than bad investing does.

Later: long-term care

Most people need some long-term care, and Medicare barely covers it. A few years in a facility can run into the hundreds of thousands. Self-fund, buy LTC insurance, or use a hybrid policy — decide before you need it.

11 — HEALTHCARE & LONG-TERM CARE
Phase 4 · Protect the plan · Stop 12

Plan for a 30-year retirement.

Longevity is the real risk

Retire at 65 and you may need income into your 90s. Plan for a long life, not an average one — running out is far worse than leaving some behind.

Inflation quietly halves it

At 3% inflation, prices double in about 24 years. Keep enough in stocks that your income keeps up — all-cash feels safe but slowly loses ground.

“The safest-feeling portfolio — all cash — is often the one most likely to run dry. Growth stays part of the plan even in retirement.”
12 — INFLATION & LONGEVITY
Phase 4 · Protect the plan · Stop 13

One plan, every piece pulling together.

Stack your income floors

Social Security and any pension are guaranteed floors. Your portfolio fills the gap on top — size it to what’s left, not the whole bill.

Coordinate the taxes

Claim age, withdrawal order, Roth conversions, and RMDs all move your tax bill. Planned together, they can save six figures over a retirement.

Revisit it yearly

Markets, tax law, and your life all change. A plan is a living thing — check it every year and adjust.

“Accounts, Social Security, taxes, and healthcare are one machine — a planner tunes them together so no single part quietly drags the rest down.”
13 — PUT THE PLAN TOGETHER
The throughline

Save it, grow it, spend it right.

Build it

Fill the right buckets

Grab the match, split Roth and Traditional, and let the HSA and decades of tax-free growth do the heavy lifting.

Size it

Know your number

Aim at roughly 25× the income your portfolio must cover — then check you’re on pace to get there.

Spend it

Make it last

A flexible ~4% withdrawal, a cash cushion, the right draw-down order, and a plan for taxes, healthcare, and a long life.

The paycheck stops, but the bills don’t. Retirement planning is just making sure the money keeps showing up — for as long as you do.
