Phase 1 · Foundation · Stop 1

Put a wall between you and the business.

Build the wall

Everything a business does — buying materials, running equipment, shipping to strangers — is a claim looking for a name. Make sure it finds the company’s, not yours.

The corporate veil

That wall is called the “corporate veil,” and it only holds if you respect it — separate money, clean records, real paperwork. Blur the line and a court can pierce it and reach your personal assets.

Form an LLC

The entity that builds the wall. Register an LLC and, kept clean, a lawsuit or debt against the business can’t reach your house, car, or savings.

“You can’t take a real swing at building something if one bad batch can take your home.”
01 — CHOOSE A STRUCTURE
Phase 1 · Foundation · Stop 2

The business’s money is not your money.

Separate accounts

Open a business checking account on day one. Revenue in, expenses out — all of it in the company’s name, never your personal account.

Don’t commingle

Mixing personal and business money is the fastest way to collapse the wall — a court can rule the company was never really separate from you, and it wrecks tax time too.

Pay yourself on purpose

Move money to yourself as a set draw or salary. Never swipe the company card for groceries.

“One account in, one account out. If a dollar can’t be traced, it can’t be defended — or deducted.”
02 — DON'T COMMINGLE
Phase 1 · Foundation · Stop 3

Build the company’s own credit — and catch every deduction.

A business card

Put spending on a card in the company’s name. It keeps things separate and builds credit under the business, not you.

Real borrowing power

Business credit unlocks bigger lines later — inventory, equipment, a delivery van — without a personal guarantee on the hook.

Catch every deduction

Categorize every expense so you deduct everything you’re owed — and pay the card in full. Interest is the priciest money a young company borrows.

“Every legitimate expense you track is money the business keeps at tax time.”

06 — RUNWAY & LIQUIDITY
Phase 3 · Protect · Stop 7

Protection is permission to take the bigger swing.

  • 1Insurance doesn’t stop bad things happening. It moves the financial loss off your balance sheet and onto the carrier’s for a monthly premium.
  • 2A defective batch, a delivery-van crash, a slip-and-fall — general and product liability turns a company-ending number into a line item.
  • 3Once a catastrophe can’t wipe you out, you can take the swings that grow a company — and it’s the swings that carry the return.
Risk you can responsibly take

Exposed: one lawsuit, one fire, or one lost partner could end it — so you play small and grow slowly to survive.

08 — BUY / SELL AGREEMENT
Phase 3 · Protect · Stop 9

Insure the people, not just the property.

A machine is replaceable in a week. The partner who runs it, and the buyer everyone trusts, are not. Two coverages guard against losing them.

Disability insurance on the partners

Death isn’t the likeliest disruption — a partner getting hurt or sick and unable to work is far more common.

  • ●A disabled partner still owns their share and still needs income — but stops contributing work.
  • ●Disability coverage replaces their income; a disability buy-out policy funds buying them out if they can’t return.

Key person insurance

Every young company has someone it can’t easily replace — at Ridgeline, the head of manufacturing who knows every machine.

  • ●The company owns a policy on that person and is the beneficiary.
  • ●If they’re lost, the payout buys time — to recruit, retrain, and cover the revenue that walks out with them.
09 — INSURE YOUR PEOPLE
Phase 4 · Scale · Stop 10

Benefits are how you win and keep good people.

Compete without overpaying

The best hires have options. Benefits let you win them without simply paying the most.

Start with the basics

Health insurance first, then dental and vision. Add group life and disability — cheap per head, and they extend the protection thesis to the whole team.

A match keeps people

A retirement match is the retention magnet. Replacing a trained employee costs far more than the benefits that kept them.

“Turnover is a tax you pay in lost knowledge. Benefits are how you stop paying it.”

11 — RETIREMENT PLANS
Phase 4 · Scale · Stop 12

The whole roadmap is one idea.

Foundation

Separate & shielded

An entity, its own accounts, its own card. A wall between you and the business, respected so it holds. Everything else is built on it.

Launch

Measured & funded

Know your margin, your cost to win a customer, and your runway. Numbers you check on a Friday, not a feeling you carry.

Protect & scale

Protected, so you can grow

Insurance on the risks, the partners, and the key people frees you to take the swings that build the company — and benefits keep the team that runs it.

Protection isn’t the cautious move. It’s what lets you stop playing small — the exposed founder plays not to lose; the protected founder plays to win.
